EXECUTIVE SUMMARY 
 
The Savannah River Site (SRS) Defense Waste Processing Facility (DWPF) processes a Monosodium 
Titanate/Sludge Solids (MST/SS) waste stream received from the Salt Waste Processing Facility (SWPF) 
via the Precipitate Reactor Feed Tank (PRFT). During processing, DWPF is required to provide evidence 
of compliance with the Waste Acceptance Product Specifications (WAPS) to ensure acceptance of their 
vitrified high-level waste (HLW) into the Civilian Radioactive Waste Management System. Production 
Records must document the constituents of the MST/SS material in the PRFT from each salt batch (StB) 
processed at SWPF. Savannah River Mission Completion (SRMC) has requested Savannah River National 
Laboratory (SRNL) to analyze PRFT samples representing each SWPF salt batch for thirty-two 
radionuclides.  
 
Additionally, elemental analysis of PRFT slurry and MST/SS solids was performed to aid SRMC in further 
refinement of the inputs and assumptions used in future frit development and Material Tracking Program 
calculations.  
 
The analyses of PRFT Batches 15, 22, and 26, which corresponds to material from the processing of StB4, 
StB5, and StB7, respectively, are reported herein. The unwashed dried solids of the PRFT batches were 
found to be 86-87% MST. Additionally, the total sulfur values are well below the assumed 982 mg of 
sulfate/kg of PRFT slurry used in Material Tracking Program calculations.

1.0 Introduction 
The Savannah River Site (SRS) Defense Waste Processing Facility (DWPF) processes a Monosodium 
Titanate/Sludge Solids (MST/SS) waste stream received from the Precipitate Reactor Feed Tank (PRFT) 
via the Sludge Solids Receipt Tank (SSRT) at the SRS Salt Waste Processing Facility (SWPF). During 
processing, DWPF is required to provide evidence of compliance with the Waste Acceptance Product 
Specifications (WAPS)1 to ensure acceptance of their vitrified high-level waste (HLW) into the Civilian 
Radioactive Waste Management System. This evidence is documented in the form of Production Records 
for DWPF canistered waste forms. 
 
Production Records must document the constituents of the MST/SS material in the PRFT from each salt 
batch (StB) processed at SWPF. In accordance with the Waste Form Qualification Report (WQR)2 for glass 
qualification, all radionuclides with a half-life longer than ten years and a curie concentration within the 
glass product of at least 0.01% of the total radionuclide activity up to 1,100 years after production must be 
reported. Within the WQR, a minimum of 30 radionuclides are reportable; however, this list can be 
expanded or reduced based on the projected curie content. In conjunction with International Atomic Energy 
Agency (IAEA) requirements, there are also other uranium and plutonium isotopes that must be included 
in the HLW analyses (e.g., U-235 and U-236). Since titanium is utilized within the MST/SS stream, its 
elemental analysis is used to determine the dilution factor for the MST/SS radionuclide calculation in the 
glass product. The analysis of the fissile uranium and plutonium isotopes and elements Fe and Mn (neutron 
poisons) in the PRFT samples also provides useful nuclear criticality safety data for the calculation of 
poison-to-fissile ratios in the MST/SS stream. Savannah River Mission Completion (SRMC) has requested 
Savannah River National Laboratory (SRNL) to analyze PRFT samples on each SWPF salt batch for the 
following thirty-two radionuclides.

The analysis of PRFT batches could also aid in further refinement of inputs and assumptions for frit 
development calculations. Currently, during sludge batch qualification, iterative Measurement Acceptance 
Region (MAR) assessments are performed based on projected compositions to identify acceptable frits for 
processing. The composition of insoluble sludge solids (ISS) in the MST/SS stream has not been previously 
measured during salt batch qualification. Instead, MAR assessments assumed the entrained ISS to be the 
composition of Sludge Batch 9 (adjusted to remove soluble species).3

In Material Tracking Program calculations, the sulfate solubility limit in glass is protected by determining 
a conservative permissible waste loading based on sample concentrations in sludge and assumed 
concentrations in the MST/SS stream. While soluble sulfate is measured during salt batch qualification, 
insoluble sulfur compounds currently are not. Analysis of the as-received PRFT samples, particularly total 
sulfur, will aid SRMC in refining the assumed sulfate concentration of the MST/SS stream. 
 
SRNL initially received PRFT Batches 15, 22, and 26, which correspond to material from the processing 
of StB4, StB5, and StB7, respectively. The analysis of PRFT batches corresponding to earlier salt batches 
will be documented in a future report.  Reported herein are the analyses of the initial received PRFT Batches 
15, 22, and 26.  
 
2.0 Experimental Procedure 
2.1 Transferring and Filtering PRFT Samples 
SRMC pulled PRFT batches 15, 22, and 26 on 04/27/22, 10/29/22, and 02/17/23, respectively. The PRFT 
samples were received at SRNL on March 2, 2023. The PRFT slurries were transferred from the 200 mL 
stainless steel dip bottles into 250 mL high density polyethylene bottles. The dip bottles were washed with 
the supernate of the PRFT samples until there were no visible solids in the dip bottles. The MST/SS solids 
were collected by filtering a portion of the PRFT slurries through a 0.45 um nylon filter. The MST/SS solids 
were not washed prior to being dried at 110°C overnight. Photos of the three PRFT slurries, Figure 2-1, 
were taken after transferring the slurries out of the dip bottles. As can be seen from the images, PRFT Batch 
26 has a peachy color, compared to the white milky color of the other PRFT batches. The different color is 
most likely due to the use of a sludge simulant filter aid during the processing of StB7 at SWPF.4

The PRFT dried unwashed solids were digested, in quadruplicate, by HNO3/HCl (aqua regia, AR)5 with 
added HF in sealed Teflon vessels. The digested sample was diluted to 100 mL with deionized water. 
Digested samples were pulled from these dilutions and submitted to Analytical and Tank Farm

and samples were submitted to ATFC for ICP-ES analysis. 
2.3.2 PRFT Slurries 
The PRFT slurries were digested, in quadruplicate, by AR/HF. The digested sample was diluted to 100 mL 
with deionized water. Digested samples were pulled from these dilutions and submitted to ATFC for ICP-
ES, ICP-MS, and Direct Mercury Analysis (DMA). Slurry samples were also digested, in quadruplicate, by 
PF.7 The slurry samples were diluted to 100 mL with deionized water and submitted to ATFC for ICP-ES 
analysis.  
 
Co, Nd, Pb, Sb, and U were determined by ICP-MS. Co was calculated from mass 59. Nd was calculated 
from the sum of masses 143 through 146, 148, and 150. Mass 142 was not included due to the potential 
interference from Ce-142. Pb was determined from the sum of masses 206, 207, and 208. Sb was determined 
from the sum of masses 121 and 123. U was calculated from the sum of masses 233-236 and 238.8 
 
The one sigma analytical uncertainty reported by ATFC was 10% for ICP-ES, DMA, and ICP-MS.

3.2 PRFT Slurry Elemental Composition  
The elemental composition results for the PRFT slurries are given in Table 3-2. The elements were 
determined from ICP-ES and ICP-MS. Results are given in mg/kg slurry. The data for Al and Si from the 
PF digestion is reported since some Al and Si species may not be soluble in an AR digestion. Due to the 
use of Zr crucibles and sodium-containing reagents (NaOH/Na2O2) in PF digestions, both Zr and Na values 
were determined from the AR/HF digestion. For all other elements quantified by ICP-ES, all digestion 
results were averaged if the difference in the results from the two digestion methods was 10% or less. If not 
within 10%, the results are taken from the digestion with the larger values. When all results are below the 
detection limit, the minimum replicate result is used. The Ca values may be biased high due to Ca in the 
blank.  For PRFT Batch 15, two of the AR replicates did not have added HF. For those two replicates, only 
Ti and S had lower values compared to the replicates with added HF. 
 
Titanium and sodium are the predominate elements in the slurry. The reported sulfur, in mg/kg of slurry, 
for PRFT Batch 15, 22, and 26 is 99.4, 80.4, and 66.9, respectively. If you assume that all the sulfur is from 
sulfate you would get  298, 241, and 200 mg/kg for PRFT batch 15, 22, and 26. These values are well below 
the assumed 982 mg/kg of sulfate in the MST/SS stream.11 The values are in line with the DWPF lab’s 
sulfate values for PRFT batch 15, 22, and 26, which are <579, <580, and <606 ppm.12 The total mercury of 
PRFT Batch 26 was also determined from the acid-digested slurry using DMA. Run in duplicate, the 
mercury value obtained was 403 mg/kg of slurry (% RSD = 0.7).

3.4 PRFT MST/SS Radionuclide Results  
The radionuclide compositions of the PRFT Batches 15, 22, and 26 are given in Tables 3-7, 3-8, and 3-9. 
The tables give the activity in µCi/g of unwashed MST/SS dried solids, the activity in pCi/g of Ti, and the 
associated relative standard deviation of the replicate analyses for each radionuclide. The percent relative 
standard deviations are given for quadruplicate analyses unless otherwise indicated. Activity measurements 
that were below the detection limit of a given technique or calculated do not have an associated relative 
standard deviation and are marked as ‘NA’ in the tables. The analytical uncertainty and method for each of 
the radionuclides is also shown. The minimum detectable activity (MDA) is the value above which the 
instrument signal can be considered quantitative relative to the signal-to-noise ratio. The upper limit is a 
measurable value above the MDA that is biased high due to spectral interference or blank contamination.  
 
Nb-93m was calculated as previously described using the Zr-93 activity and an estimated waste age of 55 
years for all three PRFT batches.6

the results appear to align with salt batch qualification results where StB7 also has a higher concentration

3.5 Slurry and MST/SS Solids Oxide Conversion 
A wt  % calcined solids can be calculated16 for the three PRFT Batches. The elements detected at greater 
than 0.01 wt % in the slurry can be converted to oxides and summed, as shown in Table 3-13. In this 
calculation, it is assumed that these elements are converted to oxides and all mercury and anions such as 
nitrite, nitrate, and hydroxide are driven off in the calcining process. Therefore, this sum represents the 
mass of oxides (calcined solids) in 100 g of total dried solids. The sum was then used to calculate the wt % 
calcined solids (slurry basis). For PRFT Batch 15, the calculated wt % calcined solids is larger than the 
wt % insoluble solids. This appears to be due to the amount of sodium in the supernate; only ~45% of the 
sodium in the slurry is from MST for PRFT Batch 15. In comparison, for PRFT Batch 22 and 26, about 
60% of the sodium is from MST. 
 
The oxide results can be normalized and compared to current SSRT estimates17 used in MAR assessments 
as shown in Appendix B. The ISS values from the PRFT batches are well below the SSRT 600 mg/L ISS 
estimate composition used, while the TiO2 wt % of the SSRT composition estimates are lower than that of 
the PRFT batches. PRFT Batches 15, 22, and 26 are from salt batches that were processed with 0.4 g/L 
MST strikes at SWPF. SWPF has since switched to MST strikes as low as 0.1 g/L. Current results should 
be compared to PRFT batches post SWPF change to lower MST.

4.0 Conclusions 
SRNL was tasked with the analyses of PRFT Batches 15, 22, and 26, which corresponds to material from 
the processing of StB4, StB5, and StB7, respectively. The elemental composition of the slurry and 
unwashed dried solids and the activity of the requested thirty-two radionuclides have been reported. The 
unwashed dried solids of the PRFT batches were found to be 86-87% MST. Additionally, the total sulfur 
values are well below the assumed 982 mg of sulfate/kg of PRFT slurry used in Material Tracking Program 
calculations.
