EXECUTIVE SUMMARY 
 
The Savannah River National Laboratory (SRNL) analyzed samples from Savannah River Site (SRS) Waste 
Tanks 41H and 21H to support qualification of Salt Waste Processing Facility (SWPF) Waste Batches 8 
and 9 for processing (the FY23 Salt Batch Qualification samples). These Tanks (i.e. 41H and 21H) are 
blend tanks for feed to SWPF.  None of the samples displayed any unusual or unexpected characteristics 
such as large amounts of solids, floating solids, or unusual color. Characterization of these samples 
confirmed similar chemical composition and characteristics to previous salt waste batches. The results for 
Batches 8 and 9 were provided by SRNL to Savannah River Mission Completion (SRMC), the Liquid 
Waste Operations subcontractor at SRS, as External Sample Results (Laboratory Information Management 
System (LIMS)) Reports. Additionally, a separate technical memo was issued by SRNL to report re-test 
data for Batch 8 for Cs-137 for filtered samples only which were run at the request of SWPF. For Batch 9, 
a set of samples was also analyzed in parallel by the SWPF-Analytical Laboratory (SWPF-AL). The SWPF-
AL data is included herein for comparison with the SRNL data where applicable. The analytical results 
(both rapid, typically 4 weeks, and long term, typically 8 weeks) for Batches 8 and 9 are now summarized 
and discussed in this technical report.  
SWPF Salt Batch 8 and 9 Qualification samples were similar in chemical composition to each other and to 
the previous 17 Salt Waste Batches (10 Interim Salt Waste Processing (ISWP) Macrobatch and 7 SWPF 
Salt Batch Qualification samples) analyzed to date with some results trending at or beyond the higher end 
of the ranges previously observed for some analytes.  Salt Batch 8 and 9 results for sodium, aluminum, and 
hydroxide were on the high end or higher than results for previous salt batches analyzed to date.  Salt 
Batches 8 and 9 contained 7.19 to 7.46 M Na+ (the previously reported maximum value for all batches was 
7.50 M Na+ for Batch 6), 0.371 to 0.437 M AlO2- (the previously reported maximum value for all batches 
was 0.368 M AlO2- for Batch 6), 3.52 to 4.07 M OH- (the previously reported maximum value was 3.78 M 
OH- for Batch 6), 1.59 to 1.98 M NO3- (previous range 1.30 to 3.27 M NO3-), 0.87 to 1.16 M NO2- (previous 
range 0.22 to 1.28 M NO2-), 0.265 to 0.313 M CO32- (previous range 0.11 to 0.39 M CO32-), 0.033 to 0.042 M 
SO42- (previous range 0.05 to 0.16 M SO42-), and other minor chemical components. Total mercury 
concentrations ranged from 40.0 to 64.1 mg/L.  These mercury values are in the middle of the range reported 
for all prior 17 Salt Batch Qualification samples (9.17 to 129.0 mg/L Hg).  
Cs-137 activities for Batches 8 and 9 were as high or higher than the maximum reported (5.22E+08 pCi/mL, 
1.16E+09 dpm/mL) for the previous 17 Salt Waste Batches analyzed to date. All Cs-137 activities, in both 
filtered and unfiltered samples of Salt Batches 8 and 9, ranged from 4.78E+08 pCi/mL (1.1E+09 dpm/mL, 
1.8 Ci/gal) to 6.22E+08 pCi/mL (1.4E+09 dpm/mL; 2.4 Ci/gal).  The Cs-137 activity for Salt Batch 8 
filtered sample was higher, 6.12E+08 pCi/mL, than the previous maximum reported 5.2E+08 pCi/mL 
(1.2E+09 dpm/mL; 2.0 Ci/gal) which was for filtered Salt Batch 6.  Revisions continue to be made to the 
SWPF Waste Acceptance Criteria (WAC) requirement related to Cs-137 content. During the time frame of 
these qualification samples, the SWPF WAC for Cs-137 content was raised to 5.1 Ci/gal but has 
subsequently been lowered to 3.0 Ci/gal (uncertainty 2 sigma applies) based on SWPF Safety Basis 
calculations related to hydrogen generation where Cs-137 value is an input. 
Sample filtration had little impact on the total gamma and beta activities for the samples indicating that 
these activities were primarily associated with soluble species.  Comparing filtered versus unfiltered 
samples, both Batch 8 and Batch 9 samples had higher activity in the unfiltered sample as compared to the 
filtered sample for Cs-137 but not significantly higher (SRNL estimated 1V as 5 %).  This trend was the 
same for most of the other radionuclide analyses as well. The total beta activity was similar to the Cs-137 
values measured. For filtered Batch 8 the total beta activity was 6.17E+08 pCi/mL (1.4E+09 dpm/mL; 
2.3 Ci/gal) and was only slightly higher than the filtered Cs-137 measured value 6.12E+08 pCi/mL (re-test 
value for four replicates).  For filtered Batch 9 the total beta activity was 5.8E+08 pCi/mL 
(1.3E+09 dpm/mL; 2.2 Ci/gal) which was only 20% higher than the Cs-137 (primary beta emitter) activities

The plutonium isotopes Pu-238, Pu-239 and Pu-240 are the major contributors to the alpha activity in SRS 
Salt Batch Feed samples, and Pu-238 accounts for the majority of the alpha activity in Salt Batch Samples 
8 and 9.  For both Batch 8 and Batch 9 analyzed by SRNL, the Pu-238 activities of the unfiltered samples 
were higher than the filtered sample by a significant amount (greater than 2Vestimated at 10% for SRNL 
analyses) indicating that not all of the Pu-238 is soluble for these batches. This is a departure in behavior 
from previous Salt Batches. Other Pu isotopes although much lower in activity than Pu-238 also exhibited 
similar behavior (Pu-239 (Batches 8 and 9) and Pu-240 (Batch 8)).  Pu-238 activity considering both filtered 
and unfiltered samples ranged in value from 6.41E+03 pCi/mL (1.4E+04 dpm/mL) for Batch 9 (filtered) to 
4.52E+04 pCi/mL (1.0E+05 dpm/mL) for Batch 8 (unfiltered).  The maximum Pu-238 activity considering 
only filtered samples in Batches 8 and 9 was 2.99E+04 pCi/mL (6.6E+04 dpm/mL) (Batch 8).  The 
maximum Pu-238 values reported previously were 8.39E+04 pCi/mL (1.9E+05 dpm/mL) for filtered (Batch 
5), and 9.14E+04 pCi/mL (2.0E+05 dpm/mL) for unfiltered (Batch 5). These activities are at or near the 
values for the total alpha with cesium removed method. The total alpha measurement following cesium 
removal is a gross semi-quantitative analysis (suffering from the high beta to alpha activity ratio in Salt 
Batch matrices) and has poor precision compared to the sum of the quantitative radiochemical analyses 
results for the various actinides. 
Comparisons of the chemical and radionuclide compositions of Salt Waste Processing Facility Batches 8 
and 9 to all of the previous 17 batches have for the most part exhibited similar compositions.  Data, where 
available, reported by SWPF-AL for Batch 9 was similar to data reported by SRNL with SWPF-AL data 
trending slightly lower for most analyses reported. The major anion concentrations and the radionuclide 
concentrations for the two batches were similar with a number of analytes generally trending higher as 
compared to previously reported Salt Batch data for the previous 17 Salt Batch samples. Although specific 
analytes, including sodium, aluminum, and hydroxide, exceeded targets included in the SWPF WAC and 
I-129 exceeded the SPF WAC limit, no significant impact was deduced from the results based on 
evaluations carried out to justify any WAC compliance.   
Note: The SWPF WAC has undergone multiple recent revisions before, during, and after FY23 including 
changes such as glycolic flowsheet addition and other adjustments including sodium and cesium 
concentrations.  This report focuses on the characterization of the Salt Batch Qualification sub-samples. 
Prior to processing for cesium removal, the Qualification Batches were transferred to process feed tanks 
which contained the heel from the previous Salt Batch. In addition, in some cases NaOH reagent was added 
to adjust the waste free hydroxide. These activities resulted in minor changes to the final waste compositions 
processed. The final compositions were estimated based on these blending and adjustment activities and 
the calculated compositions are provided in various Liquid Waste Operations subcontractor reports which 
are not summarized in this document.

1.0 Introduction 
This report provides a summary of the FY23 Salt Batch Qualification efforts and a comparison of those 
results with the previous Salt Batch Qualification results.  SRNL analyzed two Salt Batch Waste samples 
from SRS, Concentration, Storage, and Transfer Facility Tank Farms Waste Tanks 41H and 21H (Salt 
Batches 8 and 9, respectively).  SWPF-AL also performed short term analyses on Tank 21H (Salt Batch 9) 
samples.  This report details all results from SRNL analyses performed on these qualification samples 
alongside the results from SWPF-AL analyses where available. In past years, numerous Salt Waste Batch 
sub-samples have been retrieved, and this data is summarized for 17 prior Salt Waste Batches (7 Salt Batch 
and 10 Macrobatch Qualification samples) in the previous FY21 and FY22 Summary documents.i,ii Salt 
Batches were processed at SWPF, whereas the Macrobatches were processed through the Interim Salt 
Waste Processing (ISWP) system which included the Actinide Removal Process (ARP) and the Modular 
Caustic-Side Solvent Extraction Unit (MCU). 
As described in the waste acceptance criteria (WAC) document,iii the SWPF Feed acceptance criteria ensure 
that the monosodium titanate/sludge solids (MST/SS) and strip effluent streams from SWPF will not exceed 
applicable Defense Waste Processing Facility (DWPF) safety and processing constraints (e.g., canister heat 
generation, nuclear criticality safety, corrosive species, and organic concentration). The SWPF Feed 
acceptance criteria also ensures that the decontaminated salt solution (DSS) stream from SWPF will not 
exceed applicable Tank 50H and SPF safety and processing constraints (e.g., inhalation dose potential 
(IDP)), nuclear criticality safety (fissile material and fissile poisoning), radiolytic hydrogen generation, and 
organic concentration. 
A Task Technical and Quality Assurance Plan (TTQAP)iv requests SRNL to provide annual technical 
reports of the compiled Salt Batch Qualification data to Savannah River Mission Completion (SRMC), the 
current Liquid Waste Operations Sub-contractor.

2.1 Sample Receipt and Preparation 
Samples were retrieved on 11/13/2022 from SRS Tank 41H for characterization of SWPF Salt Batch 8.  
The samples were transferred from H-Tank farm and received by SRNL on 11/14/2022 for characterization 
as described in Table 2-1. Samples of Salt Batch 9 were analyzed by both SRNL and SWPF-AL.  Samples 
were retrieved from SRS Tank 21H for characterization of SWPF Salt Batch 9 as described in Table 2-1.  
The details of batch compositions, mixing and sampling for these Salt Batches have been documented in 
X-ESR-H-01164 Rev 2. v  
The Salt Batch 8 qualification sample was a composite of four 200-mL tank samples listed in Table 2-1 
which were combined into one 800-mL sample. SRNL performed all analyses for the Salt Batch 8 
Qualification sample.  
The Salt Batch 9 Qualification sample consisted of two sets of composite samples Table 2-1. The first set 
of four 200-mL samples was pulled on 5/15/2023 and delivered to SRNL on 5/16/2023. These samples 
were combined into a single 800-mL sample.  The second set of four 200-mL samples was pulled on 
5/16/2023 and delivered to the SWPF-AL on 5/17/2024.  SRNL performed both short- and long-term 
analyses on the first sample of Salt Batch 9, and SWPF-AL performed short term analyses on the second 
sample.  SWPF-AL results are included for comparison with SRNL short term results. The specific details 
of the collection of the SWPF-AL data are not included herein. Batch 9 is the first Salt Batch that SWPF-
AL has analyzed samples associated with Salt Batch Qualification.

4.0 Comparison with Previous Salt Batch Characterization Results 
SWPF Salt Batch qualification samples 8 and 9 were similar in chemical composition to each other and to 
the previous 17 salt batches analyzed to date with results trending similar to or on the higher end of the 
ranges previously observed for most analytes. Batches analyzed prior to Salt Batches 8 and 9 include ISWP 
Salt Macrobatches 1-10 and SWPF Salt Batches 1-7.i,ii SWPF-AL data reported for Salt Batch 9 is, for the 
most part, in agreement with data reported by SRNL.   
Results that were similar to previous Salt Batches included densities and insoluble solids.  For Salt Batch 8 
and 9 densities remained very close to 1.3 g/mL and the values measured for insoluble solids remained less 
than 200 mg/L.   
Salt Batch 8 and 9 results for sodium, aluminum, and hydroxide were as high (within the instrument 
uncertainty; typically 10% (1V as reported by SRNL) for these specific analyses) or higher than results for 
salt batches analyzed to date. Salt Batches 8 and 9 contained 7.19 to 7.46 M Na+ (the previously reported 
maximum value for all batches was 7.50 M Na+ for Batch 6), 0.371 to 0.437 M AlO2- (the previously 
reported maximum value for all batches was 0.368 M AlO2- for Batch 6), 3.52 to 4.07 M OH- (the previously 
reported maximum value was 3.78 M OH- for Batch 6),ii 1.59 to 1.98 M NO3- (previous range 1.30 to 3.27 
M NO3-), 0.87 to 1.16 M NO2- (previous range 0.22 to 1.28 M NO2-), 0.265 to 0.313 M CO32- (previous 
range 0.11 to 0.39 M CO32-), 0.033 to 0.042 M SO42- (previous range 0.05 to 0.16 M SO42-)  and other minor 
chemical components.  See Figure 4-1 for sodium and anion molar concentrations including SRNL data 
for Batches 5 through 9 and SWPFAL data for Batch 9.  Total mercury concentrations ranged from 40.0 to 
64.1 mg/L.  These mercury values are in the middle of the range reported for all prior 17 Salt Batches (9.17 
to 129.0 mg/L Hg).i,ii  There were a number of analyses requested as a part of the TTQAPiv that were not 
measured at values above the instrument detection limit and are not included in this report.  These analyses 
include SVOA and VOA.  
Sample filtration had little impact on the total gamma and beta activities for the samples indicating that 
these activities were primarily associated with soluble species. All Cs-137 activities, in both filtered and 
unfiltered samples of Salt Batches 8 and 9, ranged from 4.78E+08 pCi/mL (1.1E+09 dpm/mL, 1.8 Ci/gal) 
to 6.22E+08 pCi/mL (1.4E+09 dpm/mL; 2.4 Ci/gal).  The Cs-137 activity for Salt Batch 8 for the filtered 
sample was higher, 6.12E+08 pCi/mL, than the previous maximum reported 5.2E+08 pCi/mL 
(1.16E+09 dpm/mL; 2.0 Ci/gal) which was for filtered Salt Batch 6.  Comparing filtered versus unfiltered 
samples, both Batch 8 and Batch 9 samples had higher activity in the unfiltered sample as compared to the 
filtered sample for Cs-137 but not significantly higher as compared to the uncertainty of the measurement 
(typically 1Vestimated at 5% for Cs-137 by SRNL).  This observation was the same for the other 
radionuclide analyses as well. The total beta activity was similar to the Cs-137 values measured. For filtered 
Batch 8 the total beta activity was 6.17E+08 pCi/mL (1.4E+09 dpm/mL; 2.3 Ci/gal) which was higher but 
not significantly higher than the filtered Cs-137 measured value 6.12E+08 pCi/mL (re-test value for four 
replicates).  For filtered Batch 9 the total beta activity was 5.8E+08 pCi/mL (1.3E+09 dpm/mL; 2.2 Ci/gal) 
which was 20% higher than the Cs-137 (primary beta emitter) activities for this sample.  Ba-137m activities 
were calculated from the cesium data by multiplying the Cs-137 activities by 0.946 based on the fact that 
94.6% of the Cs-137 decays to Ba-137m by beta decay and a secular equilibrium exists between these 
isotopes. The total gamma reported is the calculated Ba-137m activity for each sample, since Ba-137m is 
the dominant gamma emitter in the samples. The total gamma activities for the filtered samples for Batch 
8, 5.66E+08 pCi/mL (1.3E+09 dpm/mL; 2.14 Ci/gal) are higher than Batch 9, 4.62E+08 pCi/mL 
(1.0E+09 dpm/mL, 1.75 Ci/gal), as well as  Batch 6, 4.94E+08 pCi/mL, (1.1E+09 dpm/mL; 1.87 Ci/gal).  
Revisions continue to be made to the SWPF Waste Acceptance Criteria (WAC) requirement related to Cs-
137 content.  Around the time of these qualification samples the SWPF WAC for Cs-137 content had been 
raised to 5.1 Ci/gal but has subsequently been lowered to 3.0 Ci/gal (uncertainty 2 sigma applies) based on 
SWPF Safety Basis calculations related to hydrogen generation where Cs-137 value is an input.xii

As compared to the previous 17 Batches, the trends following cesium  removal are similar for Batch 8 and 
9.  Total beta activity for the samples decreased dramatically (two orders of magnitude) to 
1.32E+06 pCi/mL (2.9E+06 dpm/mL, 5.0E-03 Ci/gal) for filtered Batch 8 and 9.59E+05 pCi/mL 
(2.1E+06 dpm/mL, 3.6E-03 Ci/gal) for filtered Batch 9 as compared to Cs-137 values.  Activities for 
radionuclides other than Cs-137 and Ba-137m in the samples were primarily associated with Sr-90/Y-90, 
Tc-99, Pu-238, and Pu-241.  Although I-129 activities are relatively low in comparison to the other 
radionuclides reported herein, the measured value for Batch 8 was higher (167 pCi/mL) than Batch 9 (93.8 
pCi/mL) and was also higher than the highest value reported for Salt Batches 1-7 (107 pCi/mL (Batch 6)).   
Although I-129 values did not exceed the SWPF WAC and do not impact the qualification of these Salt 
Batches or their processing through SWPF, additional measures are in place to reevaluate I-129 prior to 
transferring to Tank 49 as a part of an active deviation for Salt Batches 8-22 to justify exceeding the SPF 
WAC limit if it is not met at that time. 
 
Pu is the primary alpha emitter in tank waste.  The plutonium isotopes Pu-238, Pu-239, and Pu-240 are the 
major contributors to the alpha activity in SRS Salt Batch Feed samples, and Pu-238 accounts for the 
majority of the alpha activity in Salt Batch Samples 8 and 9.  For both Batch 8 and Batch 9, the Pu-238 
activity of the unfiltered sample was higher than the filtered sample by a significant amount indicating that 
not all of the Pu-238 is soluble for these Salt Batches (8 and 9). This is a departure in behavior from previous 
Salt Batches. Other Pu isotopes, although much lower in activity than Pu-238, also exhibited this behavior 
(Pu-239 (Batches 8 and 9, SRNL) and Pu-240 (Batch 8)). Pu-238 activity, considering both filtered and 
unfiltered samples, ranged in value from 6.41E+03 pCi/mL (1.4E+04 dpm/mL) for Batch 9 (filtered) to 
4.52E+04 pCi/mL (1.0E+05 dpm/mL) for Batch 8 (unfiltered).  The maximum Pu-238 activity considering 
only filtered samples in Batches 8 and 9 was 2.99E+04 pCi/mL (6.6E+04 dpm/mL) (Batch 8).  The 
maximum Pu-238 values reported previouslyi were 8.39E+04 pCi/mL (1.9E+05 dpm/mL) for filtered 
(Batch 5) and 9.14E+04 pCi/mL (2.0E+05 dpm/mL) for unfiltered (Batch 5). These activities are at or near 
the values for the total alpha with cesium removed method. The total alpha measurement following cesium 
removal is a gross semi-quantitative analysis (suffering from the high beta to alpha activity ratio in Salt 
Batch matrices) and has poor precision compared to the sum of the quantitative radiochemical analyses 
results for the various actinides.   
5.0 Conclusions 
None of the samples displayed any unusual or unexpected visual characteristics. In general, the FY23 Salt 
Batch Samples analyzed in Batches 8 and 9 were similar to each other in chemical and radiological 
composition and followed similar trends for filtered, unfiltered, and samples with cesium removal prior to 
analysis.  A significant difference in unfiltered and filtered values was observed, however, for Pu-238 and 
some other Pu isotopes. This observation indicates that some of the Pu species in Batches 8 and 9 may be 
insoluble. For Batch 9 analyses, the data reported by SWPF-AL was in reasonable agreement with the 
values reported by SRNL.  SWPF-AL data values trended slightly lower than SRNL measured values for 
most analyses.  Sample filtration had little impact on the total gamma and beta activities for the samples 
indicating that these activities were primarily associated with soluble species.  Values for a number of 
analytes measured in Batches 8 and 9 continued to trend toward values as high or higher than previously 
reported for the 17 previous Salt Batches (10 Macrobatch samples and 7 Salt Batch Samples). These 
analyses trending higher included sodium, aluminum, and hydroxide as well as Cs-137 (and related analyses 
for Ba-137m and total gamma). Although the concentrations and activities were higher in value and 
exceeded targets included in the SWPF WAC for sodium, aluminum, and hydroxide and the SPF WAC 
limit for I-129, no significant impact was deduced from the results based on evaluations carried out to 
justify any WAC compliance.

6.0 Recommendations, Path Forward or Future Work 
In the next summary document, the average values and ranges for key measured analytes for all Salt Batches 
reported to date through Salt Batch 9 (10 Macrobatch plus 9 Salt Batch samples) should be updated in order 
to be compared with the FY24 Salt Batch sample data.  A methodology has not yet been fully developed 
for addressing final calculated compositions of the wastes processed through the ISWP facility and the 
SWPF which takes into account the impacts of the process feed tank heel compositions and added NaOH 
reagent.  Once developed, this methodology should be considered in future revisions of this report.  Also, 
a comparison of the final blended Salt Batch feed composition and the corresponding Tank 50H 
decontaminated salt solution may be considered to complement future summaries.
