EXECUTIVE SUMMARY 
 
Savannah River Mission Completion (SRMC), the Liquid Waste Operations subcontractor at SRS, and 
Savannah River National Laboratory (SRNL) analyzed samples from Savannah River Site (SRS) Waste 
Tanks 21H, 41H, and 42H to support qualification of Salt Waste Processing Facility (SWPF) Waste Batches 
10, 11, 12, and 13 for processing (the FY24 Salt Batch Qualification samples). These Tanks (i.e. 21H, 41H 
and 42H) are blend tanks for feed to SWPF.  This report focuses on the characterization of the Salt Batch 
Qualification sub-samples (i.e., any adjustments after qualification or as a part of processing are 
documented elsewhere).  None of the samples displayed any unusual or unexpected characteristics such as 
large amounts of solids, floating solids, or unusual color. Characterization of these samples confirmed 
similar chemical composition and characteristics to previous salt waste batches.  The analytical results (both 
rapid, typically 4 weeks, and long-term, typically 8 weeks) for Salt Batches 10, 11, 12, and 13 are now 
summarized and discussed in this technical report.  SRMC-Analytical Laboratory (SRMC-AL), for the first 
time, provided all the short-term analyses data used in the qualification evaluations of SWPF Salt Batches 
10, 11, 12, and 13.  These SRMC short-term results are presented along with the results for the long-term 
analyses provided by SRNL. 
 
SWPF Salt Batch 10, 11, 12, and 13 Qualification samples were similar in chemical composition to each 
other and to the previous 19 Salt Waste Batches (10 Interim Salt Waste Processing (ISWP) Macrobatch and 
9 SWPF Salt Batch Qualification samples) analyzed to date with some results trending at or beyond the 
higher end of the ranges previously observed for some analytes.  Salt Batch 10, 11, 12 and 13 densities 
remained very close to 1.3 g/mL and the values measured for insoluble solids remained less than 200 mg/L 
for all batches except Batch 11 which was 550 mg/L and contained visible solids which settled with time.  
Salt Batch 10, 11, 12 and 13 results for sodium (6.82 to 8.26 M), aluminum (0.274 to 0.356 M), and 
hydroxide (3.12 to 3.85 M) continue to trend toward the high end or higher than results for salt batches 
analyzed to date (previous maximum to date 7.50 M (Batch 6) for sodium, 0.437 M (Batch 8) for aluminum, 
and 4.07 M (Batch 8) for hydroxide. Sodium and fluoride in Batch 12 (8.26 M and 0.00616 M, respectively) 
were measured at values higher than the previous maximum values for those analytes reported for Salt 
Batch Qualification samples.  Total mercury values (38.0 to 50.0 mg/L) were in the middle of the range 
previously reported (9.17 to 129.0 mg/L). Sulfate values for Salt Batches 11 and 12 (0.0233 M and 0.0316 
M, respectively) were lower than the previous range (0.05 M to 0.16 M) for all previous Salt Waste Batches.  
Cs-137 activities in filtered samples of Salt Batches 10, 11, 12 and 13 ranged from 3.65E+08 pCi/mL 
(8.1E+08 dpm/mL; 1.4 Ci/gal) to 5.73E+08 pCi/mL (1.3E+09 dpm/mL; 2.2 Ci/gal).  The previous 
maximum value was for unfiltered Salt Batch 8 (6.22E+08 pCi/mL (1.4E+09 dpm/mL; 2.4 Ci/gal)).  These 
values remain below the current WAC value of 3.0 Ci/gal.  The total beta activity was similar to the Cs-
137 values measured.  In comparison of filtered and unfiltered results where available for the various 
radionuclide measurements, typically the filtered samples values measured were the same or lower than the 
unfiltered. For Batch 11 there were larger differences between filtered and unfiltered sample results likely 
owing to the presence of solids with which the radionuclides may be associated.  I-129 (<45.5 pCi/mL to 
155 pCi/mL) and Tc-99 (9.38 E+04 to 1.52E+05) values continue to trend near the WAC limits for the 
Saltstone Production Facility (SPF).  Although the concentrations and activities continue to trend on the 
high end or higher than the SWPF WAC for sodium, aluminum, hydroxide, and Cs-137 (and related 
analyses for total gamma) as well as the downstream WAC limit for I-129 and Tc-99, no significant impact 
was deduced from the results based on evaluations carried out to justify any WAC deviations.

1.0 Introduction 
This report provides a summary of the FY24 Salt Batch Qualification efforts and a comparison of those 
results with the previous Salt Batch Qualification results.i, ii, iii  SRNL and SRMC analyzed four Salt Batch 
Waste samples from SRS, Concentration, Storage, and Transfer Facility Tank Farms Waste Tanks which 
included one Salt Batch Qualification sample from 41H (Salt Batch 10), one from 21H (Salt Batch 11), and 
two from 42 H (Salt Batches 12 and 13).  SWPF-AL, for the first time, performed all short-term analyses 
used in the qualification of these four salt batches.  SRNL performed the long-term analyses.  This report 
details all results from SRMC and SRNL analyses performed on these qualification samples. In past years, 
numerous Salt Waste Batch sub-samples have been retrieved, and this data is summarized for 19 prior Salt 
Waste Batches (9 Salt Batch and 10 Macrobatch Qualification samples) in the previous FY21 FY22, and 
FY23 Summary documents.i,ii,iii  Salt Batches were processed at SWPF, whereas the Macrobatches were 
processed through the Interim Salt Waste Processing (ISWP) system which included the Actinide Removal 
Process (ARP) and the Modular Caustic-Side Solvent Extraction Unit (MCU). 
As described in the waste acceptance criteria (WAC) document,iv the SWPF Feed acceptance criteria ensure 
that the monosodium titanate/sludge solids (MST/SS) and strip effluent streams from SWPF will not exceed 
applicable Defense Waste Processing Facility (DWPF) safety and processing constraints (e.g., canister heat 
generation, nuclear criticality safety, corrosive species, and organic concentration). The SWPF Feed 
acceptance criteria also ensures that the decontaminated salt solution (DSS) stream from SWPF will not 
exceed applicable Tank 50H and Saltstone Production Facility (SPF) safety and processing constraints (e.g., 
inhalation dose potential (IDP)), nuclear criticality safety (fissile material and fissile poisoning), radiolytic 
hydrogen generation, and organic concentration. 
A Task Technical and Quality Assurance Plan (TTQAP)v requests SRNL to provide annual technical reports 
of the compiled Salt Batch Qualification data to Savannah River Mission Completion (SRMC), the current 
Liquid Waste Operations Sub-contractor. 
2.0 Experimental Procedure 
2.1 Sample Receipt and Preparation 
Samples of Salt Batch 10, 11, 12, and 13 were analyzed by both SRNL and SWPF-AL as described in 
Table 2-1.  Typically, the first in the series of five 200-mL samples retrieved was transferred from H-Tank 
farm to SRNL.  An additional four 200-mL samples were also retrieved either the same day or the next day.  
This second set of four samples was delivered to SWPF-AL.  Transfer of samples from H-Tank farm to the 
respective lab was typically either the same day as the sample was retrieved or the next day but not longer 
than 2 days.   
The details of batch compositions, mixing and sampling for these Salt Batches have been documented in 
X-ESR-H-01164 Rev 4.vi  The chronology of sampling and analysis for the various batches reported herein 
did not necessarily follow the numerical sequence for the salt batches.  Samples were retrieved on 10/2/2023 
and 10/3/2023 from SRS Tank 42H for characterization of SWPF Salt Batch 12.  Samples were retrieved 
on 12/11/2023 and 12/12/2023 from SRS Tank 41H for Salt Batch 10.  Samples were retrieved on 3/11/2024 
and 3/12/2024 from Tank 21H for Salt Batch 11.  Samples were retrieved on 6/10/2024 and 6/11/2024 from 
Tank 42H for Salt Batch 13. 
SWPF-AL performed all short-term (four-week) analyses for the Salt Batch 10, 11, 12 and 13 qualification 
samples. The Salt Batches 10, 11, 12 and 13 qualification samples analyzed by SWPF-AL were composite 
samples of four 200-mL tank samples listed in Table 2-1 which were combined into one 800-mL sample. 
SRNL provided additional long-term (eight-week) analyses on a 200-mL sample pulled in the same 
timeframe as the respective composite 800-mL sample analyzed by SWPF-AL (see Table 2-1)

For the analyses conducted in SRNL, the details of the analyses are as follows.  For samples requiring 
filtration, filtration was conducted using a 0.45-μm nylon syringe filter and the filtrate was submitted for 
analysis.  For the methyl mercury measurements, approximately 1.5-mL sub-samples (unfiltered) were 
diluted into 40 mL of ultrapure water acidified with 0.2 mL of ultrapure HCl prior to adding the 1.5 mL of 
sub-sample. Dilution was carried out in Teflon-capped, glass vials with very little headspace and the diluted 
samples were removed from the shielded cells and stored below 4 °C (in a refrigerator), following the 
recommended method for preparing samples of this type.xv  For iodine analyses, aliquots of as-received 
sample were analyzed directly, and the separations were traced with an elemental iodine carrier from the 
initial aliquoting step. Samples were analyzed in duplicate or triplicate (re-test Batch 11) with a blank.  The 
blank values were below the detection limit.  The detection limit was less than 0.5% of the measured values 
in the samples.

3.0 Characterization Results for the FY24 SWPF Salt Batches (10, 11, 12 and 13) including 
analyses by SRNL and SWPF-AL 
SWPF-AL measured and reported density of unfiltered samples (Table 3-1).  SWPF-AL analyzed filtered 
samples for major ions including hydroxide, carbonate, formate, nitrate, nitrite, sulfate, phosphate, oxalate, 
chloride, and fluoride as well as elemental aluminum, sodium, potassium, and silicon (Table 3-1).  SWPF-
AL also reported TOC on filtered samples and total mercury analyses on unfiltered samples (Table 3-1).  
SRNL performed methyl mercury analyses which were performed on unfiltered samples (Table 3-1).  
Radioisotopes analyzed with results determined to be above the instrument detection limit are listed in 
Table 3-2 and Table 3-3.  Short-term analyses (Cs-137, gamma , beta and alpha measurements, U isotopes, 
Pu isotopes except for Pu-241) were performed by SWPF-AL, and the remaining analyses (long-term) were 
performed by SRNL on filtered samples.  A number of additional radionuclide analyses were carried out as 
specified in the TTQAP. v  Those radionuclides below the limit of detection for Salt Batches 10, 11, 12, and 
13 were not included in this summary report. Analytes measured but not included in Table 3-2 and 
Table 3-3 are Cm-245, Ce-144, Co-60, Eu-154, Eu-155, and Sb-125.  Detection limits for all analyses were 
below the Waste Acceptance Criteria (WAC) limits.   
SRNL utilized automated Laboratoy Information Management System (LIMS) data reporting for the initial 
reporting of the long-term analyses data for Salt Batches 10 and 12.vi,viii  A re-test was requested of SRNL 
for Salt Batch 11 to remeasure I-129 in filtered samples starting with new aliquots of original sample.  This 
re-test was reported in a separate document.vii The re-test data reported herein is an average of three values 
measured.  
Both SWPF-AL and SRNL data were used in the evaluation included in the Acceptance Report for Salt 
Batches 10, 11, 12, and 13.

4.0 Comparison with Previous Salt Batch Characterization Results 
SWPF Salt Batch qualification samples 10, 11, 12 and 13 were similar in chemical composition to each 
other and to the previous 19 salt batches analyzed to date with results trending similar to or on the higher 
end of the ranges previously observed for most analytes. Batches analyzed prior to Salt Batches 10, 11, 12, 
and 13 include ISWP Salt Macrobatches 1-10 and SWPF Salt Batches 1-9.i,ii 
Results that were similar to previous Salt Batches included densities and insoluble solids.  For Salt Batch 
10, 11, 12, and 13, densities remained very close to 1.3 g/mL.  The values measured for insoluble solids 
remained less than 200 mg/L for all batches with the exception of Batch 11, which was 550 mg/L and 
contained visible solids which settled with time.  These solids amounts were still well below the WAC for 
SWPF.   
Salt Batch 10, 11, 12, and 13 results for sodium, aluminum, and hydroxide were as high for these specific 
analyses or higher than results for salt batches analyzed to date. Salt Batches 10, 11, 12, and 13 contained 
6.82 to 8.26 M Na+ (the previously reported maximum value for all batches was 7.50 M Na+ for Batch 6), 
0.274 to 0.356 M AlO2- (the previously reported maximum value for all batches was 0.437 M AlO2- for 
Batch 8), 3.12 to 3.85 M OH- (the previously reported maximum value was 4.07 M OH- for Batch 8),ii 1.74 
to 2.52 M NO3- (previous range 1.30 to 3.27 M NO3-), 0.77 to 1.09 M NO2- (previous range 0.22 to 1.28 M 
NO2-), 0.172 to 0.283 M CO32- (previous range 0.11 to 0.39 M CO32-), and other minor chemical 
components.  Sulfate concentrations were measured in the range 0.0233 to 0.0675 M SO42.  Sulfate values 
for Batches 11 and 12 were lower than the previous range (0.05 to 0.16 M SO42-).  See Figure 4-1 for sodium 
and anion molar concentrations including SRNL data for Batches 5 through 9 and SWPF-AL data for 
Batches 9 through 13.  For Batch 9, both SRNL and SWPF-AL data are show in Figure 4-1. Data for Salt 
Batches 5-9 included in Figure 4-1 is found in previous Salt Batch Qualification Summary Reports.ii,iii  
Total mercury concentrations ranged from 38.0 to 50.0 mg/L.  These mercury values are in the middle of 
the range reported for all prior 19 Salt Batches (9.17 to 129.0 mg/L Hg).i,ii,iii  Methyl mercury values 
measured were highest for Salt Batch 13 (2.57E+01 mg/L methyl mercury).  Methyl mercury for Salt Batch 
13 constituted a significant fraction of the total mercury measured (3.80E+01 mg/L total mercury) in 
comparison to the other three batches which all had higher values measured for total mercury than Batch 
13 but lower methyl mercury measured values.

Cs-137 activities in filtered samples of Salt Batches 10, 11, 12, and 13 ranged from 3.65E+08 pCi/mL 
(8.1E+08 dpm/mL; 1.4 Ci/gal) to 5.73E+08 pCi/mL (1.3E+09 dpm/mL; 2.2 Ci/gal).  The previous 
maximum value was for unfiltered Salt Batch 8 (6.22E+08 pCi/mL (1.4E+09 dpm/mL; 2.4 Ci/gal)).  Sample 
filtration had little impact on the total gamma and beta activities for the samples indicating that these 
activities were primarily associated with soluble species.  When comparing filtered versus unfiltered 
samples’ radionuclide measurements, typically measured values for unfiltered samples were higher than or 
the same as (within the uncertainty of the measurement) the measured value for the same filtered sample.  
This observation was most prominent for Salt Batch 11, which also had measurable solids suggesting that 
some radionuclides may be associated with the solids in this batch. The total beta activity was similar to 
the Cs-137 values measured.  Ba-137m activities can be calculated from the cesium data by multiplying the 
Cs-137 activities by 0.946 because 94.6% of the Cs-137 decays to Ba-137m by beta decay and a secular 
equilibrium exists between these isotopes.  The total gamma reported is lower than the calculated Ba-137m 
values for Batches 10, 11, 12, and 13.  The total gamma activities for the filtered samples for Batches 10, 
11, 12, and 13 continued to trend on the higher end of the range measured to date (4.6E+07 pCi/mL to 
5.66E+08 pCi/mL (1.3E+09 dpm/mL; 2.14 Ci/gal)).  Revisions continue to be made to the SWPF Waste 
Acceptance Criteria (WAC) requirement related to Cs-137 content.  Around the time of these qualification 
samples, the SWPF WAC for Cs-137 content had been raised to 5.1 Ci/gal but has subsequently been 
lowered to 3.0 Ci/gal (uncertainty of two sigma applies) based on SWPF Safety Basis calculations related 
to hydrogen generation where Cs-137 value is an input.xvii  The current SWPF WAC limit for Cs-137 is 3.0 
Ci/gal.iv  The Cs-137 values for Salt Batches 10,11, 12, and 13 remained below the current WAC value of 
3.0 Ci/gal.   
There were a number of analyses requested as a part of the TTQAPv that were not measured at values above 
the instrument detection limit and are not included in this report.  These analyses include Cm-245, Ce-144, 
Co-60, Eu-154, Eu-155, and Sb-125.

As compared to the previous 19 Batches, the trends following cesium removal are similar for Batches 10, 
11, 12, and 13.  Total beta activity for the samples decreased dramatically (two orders of magnitude) as 
compared to Cs-137 values.  Activities for radionuclides other than Cs-137 and Ba-137m in the samples 
were primarily associated with Sr-90/Y-90, Tc-99, Pu-238, and Pu-241.  Although I-129 activities (<45.5 
pCi/mL to 155 pCi/mL) are relatively low in comparison to the other radionuclides reported herein, the 
measured value for Batch 11 (155 pCi/mL) was the highest value measured for batches 10 through 13 and 
close to the previous maximum measured for Batch 8 (167 pCi/mL).  I-129 and Tc-99 (9.38E+04 pCi/mL 
to 1.52E+05 pCi/mL) values continue to trend near the WAC limits for the Saltstone Production Facility 
(SPF) which are currently 1.00E+02 and 2.11E+05 pCi/mL, respectively.xviii 
Although I-129 values did not exceed the SWPF WAC and do not impact the qualification of these Salt 
Batches or their processing through SWPF, additional measures are in place to reevaluate I-129 prior to 
transferring to Tank 49 as a part of an active deviation for Salt Batches 8-22 to justify exceeding the SPF 
WAC limit if it is not met at that time.  
Pu is the primary alpha emitter in tank waste.  The plutonium isotopes Pu-238, Pu-239, and Pu-240 are the 
major contributors to the alpha activity in SRS Salt Batch Feed samples, and Pu-238 accounts for the 
majority of the alpha activity in Salt Batch Samples 10, 11, 12, and 13.  Pu-238 activity, of unfiltered 
samples, ranged in value from 1.67E+04 pCi/mL (3.71E+04 dpm/mL) for Batch 13 to 8.70E+04  pCi/mL 
(1.93E+05 dpm/mL) for Batch 10.  The maximum Pu-238 values reported previouslyii were 8.39E+04 
pCi/mL (1.9E+05 dpm/mL) for filtered (Batch 5) and 9.14E+04 pCi/mL (2.0E+05 dpm/mL) for unfiltered 
(Batch 5). For Pu isotopes, where both unfiltered and filtered samples were measured, the unfiltered sample 
was higher than the filtered sample; this difference was most significant for Batch 11 where solids were 
observed.  These activities are at or near the values for the total alpha with cesium removed method. The 
total alpha measurement following cesium removal is a gross semi-quantitative analysis (suffering from the 
high beta to alpha activity ratio in Salt Batch matrices) and has poor precision compared to the sum of the 
quantitative radiochemical analyses results for the various actinides. 
5.0 Conclusions 
None of the samples displayed any unusual or unexpected visual characteristics. In general, the FY24 Salt 
Batch Samples analyzed in Batches 10, 11, 12, and 13 were similar to each other in chemical and 
radiological composition and followed similar trends for filtered, unfiltered, and samples with cesium 
removal prior to analysis.  Total beta measurements with cesium removed unfiltered and filtered values 
were similar with the exception of Batch 11 where the presence of solids could be attributed to the difference 
in these unfiltered and filtered values.  For the Pu and U isotopes and total alpha analyses where both 
unfiltered and filtered values were measured, a significant difference in unfiltered versus filtered values 
was observed in general and was greatest for Batch 11 (either close to or over an order of magnitude for 
Pu-239, Pu-240, total alpha, and Sr-90 measurements).  These observations indicate that some of these 
species in Batch 11 may be insoluble and associated with the insoluble solids which were measured to be 
higher in Batch 11 than in Batches 10, 12, or 13.  Sample filtration had little impact on the total beta without 
cesium activities for the samples indicating that these activities were primarily associated with soluble 
species except for Batch 11 which contained higher solids.  Values for a number of analytes measured in 
Batches 10, 11, 12, and 13 continued to trend toward values as high or higher than previously reported for 
the 19 previous Salt Batches (10 Macrobatch samples and 9 Salt Batch Samples). These analyses trending 
higher included sodium, aluminum, and hydroxide as well as Cs-137 (and related analyses for total gamma). 
Although the concentrations and activities were higher in value and exceeded targets included in the SWPF 
WAC for sodium, aluminum, and hydroxide and the SPF WAC limit for I-129, no significant impact was 
deduced from the results based on evaluations carried out to justify any WAC deviations.

6.0 Recommendations, Path Forward, or Future Work 
In the next summary document, the average values and ranges for key measured analytes for all Salt 
Batches reported to date through Salt Batch 13 (10 Macrobatch plus 13 Salt Batch samples) should be 
updated in order to be compared with the FY25 Salt Batch sample data.   
Sulfate values for several batches (11 and 12) were noted to be lower than the previous range observed for 
all salt batches.  Precipitation of insoluble sulfate species (such as kogarkoite) is anticipated at higher 
sodium concentration.  OLI modeling can be used to further explore the behavior of various insoluble 
species as a function of changing sodium or other ion concentrations to predict the behavior of insoluble 
species in salt batch heels. 
Similar to this annual summary report for the Salt Batch Qualification feed, a periodic summary 
document to consolidate and summarize DSS data would be useful as a consolidated reference for 
comparison of the DSS data with the salt batch data in the Annual Salt Batch summary documents.
