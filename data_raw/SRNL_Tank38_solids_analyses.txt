EXECUTIVE SUMMARY 
 
 
The October 2023 Tank 38 HTF-38-23-96 VDS was reported to contain 3.0 ± 0.1 wt% insoluble solids and 
warm acid strike dissolution indicated presence of relative high amounts of High-Level Waste (HLW) 
sludge components such as Al, Ca, Fe, Mn and U.  Savannah River Mission Completion (SRMC) personnel 
thus sampled the Tank 38 in early February 2024 to provide the HTF-38-24-21 VDS for insoluble solids 
analysis at the Savannah River National Laboratory (SRNL).  This report presents results for the elemental, 
radionuclide and crystalline species analyses of the February 2024 HTF-38-24-21 washed and dried solids.   
 
The wt% insoluble solids measured for the HTF-38-24-21 VDS sample is 6.1 ± 0.7 wt%.  Major elements 
detected in the washed and dried Tank 38 solids are Al and Na at over 10 wt%, followed by Ca, Fe, Mn, Si 
and U in the range of 1.6 to 8.7 wt%.  Trace levels of Ba, Zn and Zr are detectable at ~ 0.02 wt% with 
higher levels of Cr (0.12 wt%), Ni (0.25 wt%) and Mg (0.46 wt%) present.  Some elements were not 
detectable including B, Gd, Li and Ti.  Comparison of the Tank 38 solids elemental concentrations to recent 
Sludge Batch 9 (SB9) dried sludge slurry indicates that the Tank 38 solids compare similarly with most 
sludge species except that the Na/Fe, Al/Fe and Si/Fe ratios for the Tank 38 solids are all significantly 
higher than those respective ratios in SB9 sludge.  The SB9 sludge composition is deemed the most 
reasonable sludge composition for comparison to the February 2024 Tank 38 sample since no transfers 
have been made from Tank 22 to Tank 38 since January of 2023 and processing of SB10 did not start until 
March of 2023.  Comparison of the typical sludge species Mn, Ni and U to Fe ratios for either the HTF-38-
24-21 solids (Mn/Fe = 0.4, Ni/Fe = 0.04, U/Fe = 0.9) or the SB9 slurry (Mn/Fe = 0.33, Ni/Fe = 0.07, U/Fe 
= 0.2) indicate similar respective ratios suggesting that the insoluble solids in the HTF-38-24-21 sample are 
indeed sludge-derived solids.  One exception to this observation is the higher total uranium levels (either 
as ratio to iron, or as wt% solids) in the Tank 38 solids vs. sludge.     
 
Similar comparison of the Tank 38 solids elemental concentrations was made to recent SB9 Defense Waste 
Processing Facility (DWPF) pour stream glass elemental concentrations.  Several of the Tank 38 elementals 
are higher than SB9 glass (Al, Ca and U).  Many of the Tank 38 elementals are similar to those in glass (Fe, 
Mn, Na, Zn and Zr).  However, the Si in Tk 38 is ~ 2.6X lower compared to SB9 glass (8.7 wt% vs. 23 
wt%).  Additionally, several of the glass elementals (B, Li and Ti) are not detectable in the Tk 38 solids.  
These data indicate that the Tank 38 solids are not consistent with the elemental signature expected from 
SB9 pour stream glass. 
 
A suite of radionuclides was also analyzed for the Tank 38 solids.  These radionuclides including isotopes 
of U and Pu and Am/Cm, as well as Cs-137 and Sr-90 are noted to be in similar relative abundance to those 
of SB9 sludge.  One exception is the higher total uranium wt% in the Tank 38 solids relative to SB9 sludge.  
 
Crystalline species analyses on the washed and dried Tank 38 solids indicates presence of various sodium 
aluminosilicate (NAS) species similar to analogous measurements performed in 2004, 2015 and 2016 on 
Tank 38 solids.  From consideration of the elemental, radionuclide and crystalline species data presented in 
this report, the Tank 38 solids are judged to be most likely sodium aluminosilicate solids that also contain 
insoluble sludge components.  Assigning all the measured Na, Al and Si to NAS components results in a 
calculated 64 wt% NAS and 36 wt% sludge as the estimated mass fractions.  If all of the analyzed uranium 
in the solids is associated with the NAS, then the calculated values are 75 wt% NAS and 25 wt% sludge  as 
the estimated mass fractions.

1.0 Introduction 
 
This technical report provides results for the 2024 Variable Depth Sample (VDS) from H Area Tank Farm 
Tank HTF-38-24-21 for solids characterization.  These data are needed to aid Savannah River Mission 
Completion (SRMC) in planning for operations of Tank 38, which is the drop tank for the 2H Evaporator 
and can receive Defense Waste Processing Facility (DWPF) recycle material from Tank 22.  The October 
2023 Tank 38 HTF-38-23-96 VDS was reported to contain 3.0 ± 0.1 wt% insoluble solids and warm acid 
strike dissolution indicated presence of relative high amounts of High-Level Waste (HLW) sludge 
components such as Al, Ca, Fe, Mn and U.1  Per guidance of the Task Technical and Quality Assurance 
Plan (TTQAP) for the Enrichment Control Program (ECP),2 any samples with higher than 1 wt% insoluble 
solids will be analyzed by more aggressive methods such as aqua regia or peroxide fusion dissolution of 
the washed insoluble solids.  Savannah River Mission Completion (SRMC) personnel thus sampled the 
Tank 38 in early February 2024 to provide the HTF-38-24-21 VDS, which was sampled 230.5 inches from 
the bottom of the tank, for insoluble solids analysis at the Savannah River National Laboratory (SRNL).  
Initial elemental characterization of the washed and dried filtered solids from the HTF-38-24-21 VDS have 
previously been reported.3  Previous core samples and VDS samples from Tank 38 have been analyzed to 
investigate presence of observed insoluble solids.4,5,6,7  Feed limits have been established for the 2H-
Evaporator system to ensure nuclear criticality is not possible and corrosion is minimized.8  These limits 
are protected by the Enrichment Control Program (ECP) and the Corrosion Control Program (CCP) that 
require periodic sampling and analysis to confirm that the waste supernate composition stays within the 
limits.9,10  The ECP sample characterization was requested via a Technical Task Request (TTR).11  
 
2.0 Experimental  
2.1 Technical Details and Procedures 
 
Details of the wt% insoluble solids analysis on the HTF-38-24-21 VDS were presented in the initial Tank 
38 solids characterization memorandum.3  The wt% insoluble solids measured for the HTF-38-24-21 
sample is 6.1 ± 0.7 wt%.  These washed and dried insoluble solids were subjected to both aqua regia and 
peroxide fusion digestions that used nominally 0.1 g dried solids digested up to a total of 100 mL.  These 
digested solids were analyzed by Inductively Coupled Plasma Optical Emission Spectroscopy (ICP-OES) 
for elementals characterization.  A high purity water sample was used as a reagent blank for both digestion 
methods.  The aqua regia digestions were also submitted for various radiochemical analyses.  Methods 
typically used in the ECP analyses1 were applied for uranium isotopes (separation/inductively coupled 
plasma-mass spectroscopy (S/ICP-MS) and determination of plutonium isotopes by radiochemical 
separation/extraction with thenoyltrifluoroacetone and counting methods (PUTTA chemical separation 
coupled with alpha spectroscopy).  Additionally, the digested samples were also submitted for 1) gamma 
spectroscopy to determine the Cs-137, 2) Sr-90 analysis by extraction and beta counting and 3) 
Americium/Curium (Am/Cm) radiochemical analysis by extraction and alpha spectroscopy.  Small 
milligram portions of the washed and dried HTF-38-24-21 VDS powders were also analyzed by X-Ray 
Diffraction (XRD) using a contained XRD instrument to investigate the qualitative presence of crystalline 
species.

3.0 Results and Discussion 
 
Elemental Analyses 
 
Table 3-1 provides the average Tank 38 washed and dried solids chemical analyses for the combined 
triplicate samples from both aqua regia and peroxide fusion.  Estimated one sigma percent uncertainty 
values that provide an indication of the uncertainty associated with the ICP-OES are 5%.  Some of the 
components only use aqua regia (B, Ba, Gd, Li, Na, Ti, Zn and Zr) and Si is determined by peroxide fusion 
only.  Deionized water blanks showed no detectable species except for Ca (0.3 wt%) and Mg (0.1 wt%) in 
aqua regia and Ca (0.8 wt%) in peroxide fusion.  Major metals shown are Al and Na at over 10 wt%, 
followed by Ca, Fe, Mn, Si and U in the range of 1.6 to 8.7 wt%.  Trace levels of Ba, Zn and Zr are 
detectable at ~ 0.02 wt% with higher levels of Cr (0.12 wt%), Ni (0.25 wt%) and Mg (0.46 wt%) present.  
Some elements are listed as not detectable including B, Gd, Li and Ti.  The combined average of Sludge 
Batch 9 (SB9) dried slurry elemental analyses15 are shown in the next to last column of Table 3-1 for 
comparison to the HTF-38-24-21 solids.  There have been no transfers from Tank 22 to Tank 38 since 
January of 2023 and processing of SB10 sludge did not start until March of 2023, so the SB9 sludge 
composition should be the most reasonable comparison.  The highest elemental components of SB9 sludge 
slurry are Fe (16 wt%) and Na (14.7 wt%) at over 10 wt%, with Al, Mn, Ni, Si and U in the 1.1 to 6.3 wt% 
range.   
 
Comparison of the individual components to the Fe content for either HTF-38-24-21 or SB9 slurry indicates 
that the Al, Na and Si are elevated in the Tank 38 solids vs. their respective ratios to Fe in the SB9 slurry.  
The Al/Fe, Na/Fe and Si/Fe ratios in the Tank 38 solids are 1.7, 1.8 and 1.4, respectively.  This same 
comparison for SB9 sludge slurry shows lower Al/Fe, Na/Fe and Si/Fe ratios of 0.4, 0.9 and 0.1, 
respectively.  One explanation for the increased levels of Na, Al and Si in the Tank 38 solids vs. sludge 
could be the presence of sodium aluminosilicate solids.  These crystalline species have previously been 
identified in Tank 38 solids analyses reported in 2004.4  This observation is also supported by the current 
XRD analyses of the HTF-38-24-21 solids sample as discussed below in the XRD analyses section.  
Comparison of the typical sludge species Mn, Ni and U to Fe ratios for either the HTF-38-24-21 solids 
(Mn/Fe = 0.4, Ni/Fe = 0.04, U/Fe = 0.9) or the SB9 slurry (Mn/Fe = 0.33, Ni/Fe = 0.07, U/Fe = 0.2) indicate 
similar respective ratios suggesting that the insoluble solids in the HTF-38-24-21 sample are indeed sludge-
derived solids.  One exception to this observation is the higher total uranium levels (either as ratio to iron, 
or as wt% solids) in the Tank 38 solids vs. sludge.  The HTF-38-24-21 solids analyses shown in Table 3-1 
can also be compared to the elemental analyses from recent SB9 DWPF pour stream glass.16  The elemental 
composition of the SB9 glass is shown in the last column of Table 3-1.  Several of the Tank 38 elementals 
are higher than SB9 glass (Al, Ca and U).  Many of the Tank 38 elementals are similar to those in glass (Fe, 
Mn, Na, Zn and Zr).  However, the Si in Tk 38 is ~ 2.6X lower compared to SB9 glass (8.7 wt% vs. 23 
wt%).  Additionally, several of the glass elementals (B, Li and Ti) are not detectable in the Tk 38 solids.  
These data indicate that the Tank 38 solids are not consistent with the elemental signature expected from 
SB9 pour stream glass or any other DWPF glasses. 
 
The measured elemental composition of the HTF-38-24-21 sample can also be compared to previous Tank 
38 solids analyses.6,7  If one assumes that all of the Na, Al and Si in these various solids are associated with 
sodium aluminosilicate phases, then the fraction of NAS to total measured elemental solids in these samples 
is estimated to be 76 wt% NAS,6 67 wt% NAS,7 and 64 wt% NAS for the current sample.  The remaining 
elemental solids in these samples to make up 100 wt% are sludge-containing components such as Ca, Fe,

Crystalline Species Analyses 
 
Milligram quantities of the Tk 38 solid powders were analyzed for crystalline species for 3 separate 
triplicate aliquots obtained from the parent sample jar using XRD and data collection times of ~ 3 hrs.  The 
results of the XRD analyses on the three separate powder samples are shown in Figure 3-1, Figure 3-2 and 
Figure 3-3.  The XRD patterns are similar for all three samples that show a broad amorphous ‘hump’ 
centered around the two-theta degrees value of ~ 25.  This broad amorphous hump is typically seen for 
High Level Waste (HLW) amorphous silicate glass powder samples.19   
 
All patterns contain similar magnitude peaks indicating various crystalline species present.  The [00-054-
0628] sodium aluminosilicate cancrinite containing nitrate, the hematite and the sodium aluminosilicate 
natrolite are common to all three samples.  Samples 2 and 3 also contain [00-55-1018] sodium 
aluminosilicates, and uranium oxide hydrate.  Sample 1 also contains crystalline patterns for NaNO3 and 
SiO2.  Various sodium aluminosilicates as well as hematite crystalline materials have also been identified 
in the solid core samples previously analyzed from Tank 22,20 which is the DWPF recycle tank that feeds 
into Tank 38.  Sodium aluminosilicates including hydroxycancrinite [31-1272] and Zeolite Y [434-0168] 
have also been identified in a 2004 study of Tank 38 solids.4  The sodium aluminosilicate pattern identified 
for these Tank 38 solids as the 00-054-0628 species is very similar to the crystalline pattern for the 31-1272 
hydroxycancrinite sodium aluminosilicate identified in the 2004 study.  Sodium aluminosilicate crystalline 
phases were also identified as the peaks with highest intensity in the previous 2015 and 2016 analyses.6,7
